How It Works

No cart. No algorithm. Just a process.

Ossuary Atelier is not a normal shop. Here is exactly how a piece travels from a Phuket thrift shop to your hands — and how the guide, map and tools fit around it.

The journey of a piece

Step 01

Source

We go to the shops, weekend markets and secondhand stalls across Phuket ourselves — and to Chatuchak when we can. Nothing is bought online to flip.

Step 02

Select

Only pieces with a story worth telling make the cut. If we can't say why it matters, it doesn't go in the archive.

Step 03

Research & document

For select pieces we write a story page: label, era, material, measurements and where it was found — researched and written by hand, not generated filler.

Step 04

List

The piece goes to the shop with its price and status. No countdown timers, no fake scarcity — just what is and isn't available.

Step 05

Claim & handover

Message @ossuaryatelier on Instagram. We confirm availability, then arrange pickup in Phuket or shipping.

Step 06

Afterwards

Sold pieces stay in the archive with their story intact, so the record outlives the sale.

Why there's no cart

A checkout button is convenient for the seller and anonymous for the buyer. We would rather each sale be a short conversation — so you can ask about sizing, condition or where a piece came from before you commit. It is slower, on purpose.

That also means there is no tracking pixel, no retargeting ad and no abandoned-cart email. Our privacy policy is short because there is almost nothing to say.

The guide, the map, the tools

The clothing is the front door. Behind it sits the part of the project that costs nothing and helps anyone: the Phuket Thrift Guide, an honest directory of secondhand shops; the interactive map; and free tools like the Thai slang glossary and the beach game.

They exist because the best part of secondhand is local knowledge — and local knowledge should be shared, not hoarded. If it's useful to you, the best way to say thanks is to support the project.
