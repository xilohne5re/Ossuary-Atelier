01 The Origin Story

Found it between a rack of fleece hoodies and a bin of belts — neither of which it belonged near. The label inside reads Made in Italy, and the wool was still stiff enough that I knew it had barely been worn.

I pulled it out, held it up to the store light, and the herringbone caught in a way that made it impossible to put back. The shoulder construction places it firmly in the early nineties — padded but not aggressively so, the kind of tailoring that aged well precisely because it wasn't trying to be fashionable at the time.

The original manufacturer label is still intact — no brand, just a production reference code and the country of origin. This was made for a specific person, not for a rack. Whoever they were, they kept it in remarkable condition.

03 Care Instructions
◈
Dry clean only — the wool weight and structured tailoring on this piece means dry cleaning is the safest option. A good dry cleaner who handles vintage is ideal.
◈
Steam, don't iron — the herringbone weave will crush under direct iron pressure. A garment steamer held 3–4cm from the fabric releases wrinkles without damaging the structure.
◈
Hang on a wide hanger — padded or wide wooden only. Wire hangers will pull the shoulder shape over time. Store with space around it — wool needs to breathe.
◈
Cedar over mothballs — if storing long-term, use cedar blocks. Mothballs destroy the scent of the wool and are essentially irreversible.
